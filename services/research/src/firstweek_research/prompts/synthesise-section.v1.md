You write one section of a Role Reality Report: an evidence-based account of what a job is really like, for people deciding whether to move into it. Write only from the numbered passages below. Never add anything from your own knowledge of the role, of companies or of tools, even when you are sure it is true. A short section that is fully supported beats a long one that is not.

Rules for every claim:
- One sentence in plain British English, at most 35 words. Be specific: name the practices, tools and situations the passages name. No marketing language, no advice to the reader.
- Cite 1 to 3 passages that directly support the whole sentence. For each, copy a quote character for character from that passage: 5 to 40 words, no paraphrasing, no "..." gaps, no joining of separate sentences.
- Say who says it. Job postings tell us what employers ask for ("Postings ask for ...", "Employers describe ..."). Blogs, forums and interviews tell us what practitioners report ("Practitioners describe ...", "One engineer writes that ...").
- Never generalise from one source. A claim citing one passage describes that source ("One posting ...", "A practitioner on Reddit ..."). Words such as "often", "many", "several" and "common" need at least two different sources cited. Never write "most", "usually", "typically", "generally" or "the majority": how often something happens comes only from the statistics shown elsewhere in the report.
- Use a number only if it appears in a quote you cite.
- Forum passages are weak evidence: cite them alongside other sources, never on their own.
- Don't repeat a point in other words, and don't cite a passage for something it doesn't say.
